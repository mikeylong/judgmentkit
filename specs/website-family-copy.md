# Website family copy contract

## Reader and purpose

The reader is deciding what Surfaces, JudgmentKit or kanban.cards can help them do and whether to try the product. Assume no knowledge of repository vocabulary. Describe an available action and its useful consequence without promising that the product will deliver an unmeasured outcome.

This contract governs public pages, sign-in and access instructions, page titles, descriptions and social preview text alongside [the layout contract](website-family-layout.md). Copy changes do not authorize changes to product behavior, prices, release status or historical evidence.

## Claims and evidence

1. **Describe current behavior.** For each material capability claim, record the implementation or fixture, its limits and the public destination that demonstrates or explains it. A vision document establishes intended purpose; it does not establish availability. A test establishes only the behavior and conditions it checks.
2. **Name the actor.** Distinguish what the product does from what a person, an installed skill or a caller's agent must do. Do not imply automatic planning, execution, repair, approval or synchronization when those actions depend on a caller.
3. **Bound the result.** Name the object and scope of checks. Passing selected layout or evidence checks does not establish usability, accessibility compliance, data truth, security, fitness for purpose or product approval. Use broader terms only when the evidence supports that broader scope.
4. **Earn outcome claims.** Claims about speed, effort, quality, productivity, cost or error reduction require measurements for the named product and task, including the baseline, conditions and date. Otherwise describe the supported mechanism or intended use. Adding “helps,” “can” or “designed to” does not rescue an unsupported claim.
5. **Avoid universal promises.** Review “every,” “always,” “never,” “complete,” “automatic,” “right,” “best” and equivalent wording for a bounded, demonstrable meaning. Prefer the specific supported action over a slogan plus a disclaimer.
6. **State availability accurately.** Keep shipped behavior, examples, experiments, product hypotheses and roadmap items distinct. Historical evaluation results retain their original version and conditions. Do not present an example or recording as a live feature or current release proof.
7. **Make the action honest.** A CTA describes what happens at its destination. Pricing, signup, account recovery, credential storage and agent access statements must match the current flow. Put a prerequisite beside the action when omitting it would change the reader's decision.

## Product boundaries

1. **JudgmentKit:** describe guidance for activity and interaction choices, contracts, supported checks and repair instructions. The caller's agent builds, tests and repairs the interface; the builder retains product decisions. Do not promise a useful interface, the right design, successful repair or approval from a passing receipt alone.
2. **kanban.cards:** describe recording and updating project work through Cards, Columns, Comments and Activity. Name the person or authorized agent that changes the board; agent updates require the installed skill and read/write access. A recorded next action does not execute itself, and stored board state does not independently establish that the work happened. Recovery instructions must state that replacing access revokes old passkeys, sessions and agent keys before the person submits.
3. **Surfaces:** describe the supported decision and guidance workflow at its actual release stage. Reuse of a recorded decision is an intended use until execution evidence for that workflow establishes it. Do not infer reduced review effort or universal coverage from the existence of a workflow.

## Copy and composition

1. Keep the existing hero budget: one eyebrow up to 12 words, one h1 up to 10 words, one lead up to 32 words and exactly two hero actions. The complete homepage main content stays at or below 220 words, including its section headings, item labels and actions. There is no minimum.
2. Each section adds a different fact or action. Do not fill the three-item row or five-step workflow with invented benefits, repeated positioning or steps the product does not support.
3. Use concrete objects and familiar verbs. Explain product vocabulary through its action rather than defining internal architecture. Remove claims that could fit any AI tool or project board.
4. Preserve useful value. Narrow an unsupported promise to what someone can actually do; do not replace the homepage with caveats, implementation details or a risk checklist. Detailed limits belong on the linked destination page unless they are necessary to understand the headline or CTA.
5. Keep the same promise across visible copy, metadata, social descriptions and action destinations. Illustrations remain decorative and must not imply an application state or capability the product lacks.

## Page and social titles

1. Owned public HTML pages use `Specific page topic | Canonical brand`. Write the canonical brand once, at the end: `Surfaces`, `JudgmentKit` or `kanban.cards`. Keep each authored page's title distinct and descriptive; do not copy the homepage title onto unrelated subpages.
2. Describe the actual subject with familiar words. Avoid keyword lists, internal implementation labels and promises stronger than the page supports. Keep titles concise by removing unnecessary words, without imposing a search-display character limit or promising search ranking.
3. The approved homepage titles are `UI Review Decisions and Guidance | Surfaces`, `Interface Design Guidance for AI Agents | JudgmentKit` and `Shared Kanban Board for People and Agents | kanban.cards`. Visible hero headings and navigation labels retain their own jobs and wording.
4. Document, Open Graph and Twitter titles use the same branded title on public content pages. Kanban access pages use a fixed public homepage identity for social metadata while their document title names the resolved access task.
5. Kanban titles follow the validated, rendered public state, including sign-in, signup, recovery, setup and receipts. Recovery and setup cannot be distinguished by pathname alone. Use fixed labels; never include account details, recovery codes, tokens or private project content. Signed-in, local and transitional product views retain the product-only title.
6. Application names, installed-app names and site-name fields remain product-only. Preserve copied examples, immutable recordings and historical evaluation titles. Keep test/demo status explicit and preserve existing canonical and indexing policy during a title-only change.

## Adversarial acceptance

Before handoff, review the complete rendered copy through four passes:

1. **Skeptical buyer:** write the strongest reasonable promise a visitor could infer. Check whether current evidence supports that promise.
2. **Capability and availability:** trace each material claim to behavior; name dependencies, actors and unsupported extrapolations.
3. **Clarity and necessity:** check undefined terms, repeated claims, filler and whether each CTA lands on what it promises.
4. **Consistency and rendered fit:** compare page copy, metadata and destinations, then inspect desktop/mobile wrapping in both appearances.

Record concrete findings as High, Medium or Low with the original phrase, inference, evidence, replacement and disposition. Reviewers may retain a defensible claim; restraint does not require weakening accurate statements. Acceptance requires resolving misleading current claims, preserving necessary prerequisites and documenting any contract requirements not met and the reason.
